# Pieces Beyond Balls Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Non-ball pieces (boxes, cylinders, tubes hollow or solid, cones, rings, compounds, CAD) on the test range,
several kinds on one field, seven past-game presets, rods to score on, and the checks that matter for them: held
("the coral slipped out of the claw at 2.3 s while claw.arm was moving"), rests on, and seated depth ("seated 3.5 in
of 4.0 in"), with a grip material a student picks and sweeps, a reef test field and a worked grabber example.

**Architecture:** A piece kind (`scrimmage/testrange/pieces.py`) is a ball, a disc or a rigid set of convex solids
with a mass, inertia, surface friction, long axis and optional hole. A range declares kinds in `[piece.<name>]`
tables (`[range] ball =` stays shorthand for one ball kind). The ball ledger (`Balls`) becomes one ledger over every
piece body, each slot with a kind; the ball-only work (flight forces, the shooter's ball path, goals, NT poses) runs
on ball and disc slots only, so a ball-only range steps exactly as before. A new `rod` element is what hollow pieces
slide onto. The blocks layer gains `held`, `rests_on` and `seated` conditions, a seated-depth signal, "made it in"
that counts holding, tip and turn for floor spreads and stations, and a grip sweep. Grip material is a body key in a
part's `scrimmage-edits.toml` (the overlay), applied to that body's colliding geoms as friction with a contact
priority above the piece's.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.11+, MuJoCo (MjSpec meshes from vertices, the existing ledger and contact model), numpy,
coacd (optional, already the bench's) for concave CAD pieces, `scrimmage.cad.source` for STEP/STL/OBJ, the
assembler's stdlib HTTP server and vanilla JS ES modules, Blockly (vendored), Playwright for browser tests.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-02-pieces-design.md` (approved 2026-11-02).

## Global Constraints

- Formats change only by adding optional keys. Every existing range, robot, part, test, scenario and example loads
  and runs unchanged, with the same results (a ball-only range's ledger steps bit-identically: Task 4's golden test).
- The same physics as the whole-robot simulation, no test-only shortcuts: a non-ball piece is an ordinary free
  MuJoCo body, a hollow one a ring of convex slices a rod really passes through.
- Every `[piece.*]` and `[surface.*]` preset value carries a `# source:` or `# estimate:` comment; range files keep
  their `# source:` / `# choice:` comments. Friction and squeeze values are estimates; a run that uses a grip says
  UNCALIBRATED and never presents a grip as measured.
- Numbers the spec fixes: held = lowest point at least 1 cm above anything not the robot, speed relative to the part
  under 0.05 m/s, touching it, all three for 0.1 s. Rests on = the element carries at least half the piece's weight,
  at rest (`balls.AT_REST_SPEED`, 0.02 m/s) for 0.5 s, no robot contact. Chute width without a set width: 1.3 times
  the piece's largest extent. The slip sentence names what was commanded in the second before.
- Goals count balls and discs only; shooting stays ball and disc only. Squishy non-ball pieces are rigid stand-ins.
- Plain words in everything a student reads; American spellings. Windows is first-class (`pathlib`, file replaces
  via `scrimmage.cad.settings.replace_file`); the Windows CI job runs the new non-browser tests.
- Every server or browser test sets `SCRIMMAGE_RECENT` and `SCRIMMAGE_CACHE` to temp paths. The WCAG contrast tests
  in tests/test_test_browser.py pass in light and dark with the new Setup boxes and run-panel marks shown.
- Never `uv run` in the repo. Run tests as `.venv/bin/python -m pytest ...`.

## Planning decisions

- **One ledger.** Every piece of every kind is a `ball.<i>` body in `Balls` (the name stays, so replay movers,
  `feeds[].ball`, NT and every `ball.` test keep working). The layout records each slot's kind. Per-slot masks pick
  the ball-only work: flight forces (ball form), disc forces (disc form), the carpet's rolling resistance (ball form
  and lying round rigid kinds), scoring (the kinds each goal counts), the ball path's pickup and preload (the range's
  first ball or disc kind, `Range.ball`/`Range.disc`), and `ball_poses()` (ball and disc forms). A multi-solid piece's
  geoms are `ball.<i>` (its first) and `ball.<i>.<k>`; `_bits` sets all of them.
- **A kind's frame** has its origin at its center of mass (so `Balls.position` is the COM, as for a ball), the
  inertia a full tensor about it (`fullinertia`), the long axis a unit vector in it.
- **Shapes.** `tube` takes `diameter`, `length` and an optional `inner_diameter` (hollow); `ring` is a tube read as
  short (the same keys); `cone` takes `diameter` (base), `tip_diameter` (default 0) and `length`; `cylinder` is a solid
  tube; `box` takes `size`; `sphere` takes `diameter`; `compound` lists `[[piece.<name>.shapes]]`, each a shape with
  `pos` and `rpy_deg`; `cad` takes a STEP/STL/OBJ path and `units`. Every length has its `_in` form. A hollow tube or
  ring is `TUBE_SLICES = 24` convex mesh slices, each the hull of its outer arc and its two inner corners (the hole's
  effective radius is `inner_r * cos(pi / 24)`); a solid round is a `ROUND_SIDES = 32`-gon prism or frustum mesh; a
  box a box geom; a sphere a sphere geom. A concave CAD mesh is split with `scrimmage.cad.fit.convex_split` (coacd);
  without coacd it is the bench's plain error.
- **Mass is required** for every rigid kind (the CAD readers carry no mass), with `material` (a `[surface.*]` preset)
  or `friction` (my own number) for its surface. Inertia comes from the solids at uniform density
  (`cad.mass.mesh_inertial` + `combine`) unless `inertia = [Ixx, Iyy, Izz]` is given.
- **Long axis:** a tube's, ring's, cylinder's and cone's own axis (z), a box's longest edge, a compound's or CAD's
  `axis = "x" | "y" | "z"` (default z). A kind is **flat** when its extent along the axis is less than its largest
  extent across it (gear, hatch panel, note): its words are "lying flat" (tip 90) and "standing on edge" (tip 0).
- **Orientation** `PieceKind.orient(tip_deg, turn_deg)`: the shortest turn taking the axis to +x, then pitch it up by
  tip, then yaw by turn. Range-file turns are about field +x; spread and station turns are relative to the robot's or
  station's heading. Words: tip ≥ 80 → "standing on end"; tip ≤ 10 → lying words by turn (normalized to −180..180):
  0 → "lying in line", ±90 → "lying across", 180 → "lying in line, turned around", else "lying at N°"; between →
  "leaning N°, " + the lying words. Flat kinds swap in "lying flat" / "standing on edge".
- **Resting height:** a ball as today (radius), a disc as today; a rigid piece at its lowest point plus
  `FLOOR_DROP = 0.005` m, so it settles.
- **Size words for old checks:** `PieceKind.extent` (largest bounding-box side; a ball's diameter) sizes chutes
  (1.3 × extent, walls 1.0 × extent); `PieceKind.radius` (bounding sphere) sizes the release-clear check and the
  feeder's "release point blocked" test; floor pieces are spaced by the sum of their horizontal reaches at their
  orientation. For a ball every one of these is today's number.
- **Range keys:** `[range] ball | disc | piece = "<preset>"` declares the default kind (named after its preset);
  `[piece.<name>]` adds kinds (each `preset = "<piece preset>"`, `ball = "<ball preset>"`, `disc = ...` or a
  definition). The default kind is the `[range]` one, else the first `[piece.*]`. `[[floor_balls]]`, `[[station]]`
  and `[[goal]]` gain `kind`. `[[floor_balls]]` gains `tip_deg` and `rotation_deg` (one value per table).
- **Test and config keys:** `[setup] piece` and config `[range] piece` name any ball or piece preset (the field's one
  kind replaced, on `BALL_CHOICE_RANGES` only); `ball` stays as the old spelling, both together an error. Setup writes
  `piece`; `dumps` keeps whichever key a file had.
- **Stations** gain `tip_deg`, `turn_deg` (the fed piece's base orientation, turn relative to `heading_deg`) and
  `spread_tip_deg`, `spread_turn_deg` (uniform ± limits, also in `OVERRIDE_KEYS`). A non-ball station draws 11
  uniforms per feed (the nine of today, then tip, turn); a ball station still draws nine, so its feeds are unchanged.
  The chute floor's top sits under the release point at the piece's lowest-point depth at its base orientation.
- **Rods:** `kind = "rod"` with `base`/`base_in`, `tip`/`tip_in` (x, y, z points), `diameter`/`diameter_in`,
  `section` ("round" default, or "square"), `friction`; body `element.<name>`, geom `element.<name>.rod` (a capsule
  whose rounded end reaches exactly to `tip`, or a box), welded, NON_GROUND_GROUP. A rod's length is base to tip.
- **Held** (`held = { piece = "any" | "ball.<i>", by = "<part or body>" }`): every physics step, per piece in play:
  touching a geom of `by`; no contact with a non-robot geom and its lowest point at least `HELD_CLEAR = 0.01` m above
  what is under it (a downward `mj_ray` from the lowest point, re-cast past robot hits, at most 4 casts); speed relative
  to the touched body's point velocity under `HELD_SPEED = 0.05`; all for `HELD_S = 0.1`. It stops being held at the
  first step any part fails. The how: lost touch → "slipped out of"; still touching, moving → "slid in"; touching or
  over a field geom → "was set down on <element>". What was moving: the most recent `move`/`spin` joint or `drive`/
  `turn` commanded within `SLIP_LOOKBACK_S = 1.0` before ("while claw.arm was moving", "while the robot was driving",
  "while the robot was turning"), else "while nothing was moving".
- **Seated depth** for a hollow piece and a rod (base B, tip T, axis u, length L): the hole's two ends
  E± = c ± (ℓ/2)·a. The rod runs through the hole when |a·u| ≥ cos 30° and every end with 0 ≤ (E−B)·u ≤ L lies within
  the effective inner radius of the rod's axis and at least one does. Then depth = clip(L − min s(E±), 0, could_go),
  could_go = min(L, ℓ); else empty (NaN in signals, None in JSON). Words: "seated 3.5 in of 4.0 in"; below
  `KNOCK_OFF_SHARE = 1/3` of could_go: "only 0.8 in on: could be knocked off". Signal `seated.<rod>` (inches), the
  deepest piece on that rod, listed when the field has a rod and a hollow kind.
- **Rests on** (`rests_on = { piece, on = "<element>" | "a piece" }`): the upward share of the contact forces between
  the piece and that element's geoms (or any other piece's) is at least `REST_SHARE = 0.5` of m g, the piece at rest
  for `REST_S = 0.5`, no robot contact.
- **Where a piece should end up:** `[test] hopper` keeps its key; Setup calls it "Where a piece should end up". A piece
  is "in" when its center is inside one of those bodies' boxes, or it is held by one of them at the end.
  `HOPPER_WORDS` gains "claw", "grabber", "gripper", "jaw" (`GRABBER_WORDS`).
- **Grip:** `scrimmage/parts/grip.py` holds the materials. A body's grip is `[body.<name>] grip = "<material>"` or a
  number (my own friction) in the part folder's `scrimmage-edits.toml`, for any folder part (load_part applies the
  overlay whenever the file exists; `cad.build.write_parts` now carries an existing overlay into the rebuilt folder).
  It sets every colliding, non-tread geom of that part body to that sliding friction and `GRIP_PRIORITY = 1`. A robot
  config's path `[[part]]` gains an optional `grip = { "<body>" = "<material>" | <number> }` that overrides the
  folder's: that is what the grip sweep writes into its config copy. Test files don't set grip.
- **Grabber starter:** "Test it" puts a part with a grabber-word body (and no hook body) on the `reef` field
  (`STANDIN_REEF = {"file": "reef", "start": "facing_coral"}`); its starter blocks are `wait_until held` (by the first
  grabber body, timeout 10) then `expect held` throughout with `wait 2`.
- **Replay marks:** `RunResult.marks: list[dict]` (`{"t", "kind": "slip", "text"}`) in result.json, drawn under the
  scrubber beside the feed marks. `RunResult.notes: list[str]` carries the UNCALIBRATED grip lines.

   message, and a refusal naming the extent ("the piece is 301 m across; check units"). Test in Task 1.
